      \begin{block}{Background \& Motivation}

        \heading{Cameras sample then throw away}
        A JPEG keeps only $5$--$10\%$ of DCT coefficients, and the
        ISP further reshapes the data through demosaicing,
        tone-mapping, and white-balance. By the time bits leave the
        sensor, most of what was measured is gone --- yet acquiring
        every pixel at the sensor still dominates power and shutter
        time on mobile and embedded platforms.

        \heading{Compressed sensing reverses the order}
        Instead of sample-then-throw-away, take $M\!\ll\!N$ random
        linear measurements and reconstruct via sparsity. The CS
        framework is provably exact when the signal is sparse in some
        basis $\bm{\Psi}$ and the sensing matrix $\boldsymbol{A}$ is
        incoherent with it
        \citep{candes2006stable,donoho2006compressed} --- the same
        principle that underpins single-pixel cameras, snapshot
        hyperspectral imaging, and accelerated MRI.

        \heading{Forward model and Lasso recovery}
        With $\boldsymbol{x}{=}\bm{\Psi}\boldsymbol{c}$ and
        $\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_0\!\leq\!S$, the forward model is
        $\boldsymbol{y}{=}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}{+}\boldsymbol{w}$,
        $\boldsymbol{A}{\in}\mathbb R^{M{\times}N}$. If
        $\boldsymbol{A}$ satisfies the RIP with
        $\delta_{2S}\!<\!\sqrt 2{-}1$, the Lasso relaxation
        \[
          \boldsymbol{c}_\lambda
          =\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{c}}\;\tfrac12\|\boldsymbol{A}\bm{\Psi}\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_2^2
          +\lambda\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_1
        \]
        recovers $\boldsymbol{c}$ to within $O(\epsilon)$
        \citep{candes2006stable}. We use the 2D DCT as $\bm{\Psi}$:
        natural images concentrate $\geq\!90\%$ of their energy in
        $<\!10\%$ of low-frequency coefficients.

        \heading{Symbols at a glance}
        $\boldsymbol{s}$ -- reflectance (sparse in DCT);
        \;\;$\boldsymbol{g}$ -- illumination (smooth);
        \;\;$\boldsymbol{x}{=}\boldsymbol{s}\!\odot\!\boldsymbol{g}$ -- RAW pixels;
        \;\;$\boldsymbol{y}$ -- compressed measurements;
        \;\;$\boldsymbol{A}{\in}\mathbb R^{M\times N}$ -- Gaussian sensing matrix;
        \;\;$\bm{\Psi}$ -- 2D DCT basis;
        \;\;$\boldsymbol{c}{=}\bm{\Psi}^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{s}$ -- sparse coefficients;
        \;\;$\delta{=}M/N$ -- measurement rate;
        \;\;$\rho{=}S/M$ -- sparsity rate.

      \end{block}

      \vspace{1ex}

      \begin{block}{Problem}

        RAW sensors measure
        $\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{s}\odot\boldsymbol{g}$, the
        \emph{product} of a sparse reflectance $\boldsymbol{s}$ and a
        smooth illumination field $\boldsymbol{g}$. The conventional
        \textbf{CS-then-correct} pipeline (i) reconstructs
        $\boldsymbol{x}$ from $\boldsymbol{y}$ by enforcing $\ell_1$
        on $\boldsymbol{x}$, then (ii) divides by an illumination
        estimate (typically a column mean) to recover $\boldsymbol{s}$.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Two errors compound.}
        \textbf{Sparsity is broken at the prior:} $\boldsymbol{x}$ is
        \emph{not} DCT-sparse because pointwise multiplication by
        $\boldsymbol{g}$ smears DCT energy across coefficients;
        $\ell_1$ then over-shrinks high-illumination coefficients
        before correction ever runs. \textbf{Correction is
        shape-rigid:} a column-mean divisor copies the gradient but
        cannot restore information already shrunken away.

        \medskip
        \textbf{The sequential pipeline wastes its prior} -- every
        $\ell_1$ iteration penalises structure that wasn't really
        there to begin with. \textbf{Naive fixes don't help:}
        whitening the measurements requires knowing $\boldsymbol{g}$
        in advance, and pre-flattening assumes a parametric form
        that real scenes routinely violate.

      \end{block}

      \vspace{1ex}

      \begin{alertblock}{Proposed Solution}

        Estimate reflectance and illumination \textbf{jointly} via a
        single $\ell_1{+}$smooth-prior objective in
        $(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{g})$, solved by block-coordinate
        alternation. A $K{=}10$-layer LISTA replaces the FISTA inner
        loop for fast, learned inference.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Why joint helps.} The illumination $\boldsymbol{g}$
        is smooth (a Tikhonov prior fits it) and the reflectance
        $\boldsymbol{s}$ is sparse in DCT (an $\ell_1$ prior fits
        it). Estimating both at once lets each prior act on the
        signal it was designed for, instead of forcing one $\ell_1$
        on the compromised product $\boldsymbol{x}{=}\boldsymbol{s}\odot\boldsymbol{g}$.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Joint recovery objective.} A single
        $\ell_1{+}$smooth-prior problem in
        $(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{g})$: FISTA / LISTA on
        $\boldsymbol{c}$, closed-form ridge on $\boldsymbol{g}$.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Deep-unfolded inner solver.} $K{=}10$-layer LISTA
        \citep{gregor2010lista} with Adam, gradient clipping,
        best-checkpoint restore for stability.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Scene-dependent benchmark.} Phase transition,
        multi-image rate-distortion, LISTA-vs-classical, per-scene
        illumination ablation on $5$ natural images.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Honest failure-mode analysis.} Joint catastrophically
        fails on near-uniform scenes (moon, $-8$\,dB); traced to
        scene-energy deficiency in the first c-step, suggesting a
        deployment-time energy gate.

        \medskip
        \textbf{Headline result.} $+1.1$ to $+4.7$\,dB on textured
        scenes (cameraman, astronaut); $2.3\times$ matched iteration
        reduction from five-seed LISTA, but no CPU latency gain.

      \end{alertblock}

        \heading{Joint recovery objective}
        \textbf{Replace} the two-stage pipeline with a single objective:
        \[
          \min_{\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{g}}\;
          \tfrac12\big\|\boldsymbol{A}\,\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{pix}})
          \bm{\Psi}\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{y}\big\|_2^2
          +\lambda_c\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_1
          +\lambda_g\|\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\|_2^2.
        \]
        Non-convex jointly but \textbf{block-convex} in each variable:
        the $\boldsymbol{c}$-step is a standard Lasso (FISTA / LISTA);
        the $\boldsymbol{g}$-step is closed-form ridge.

        \heading{Block-coordinate recipe}
        Initialise $\boldsymbol{g}^{(0)}{=}\boldsymbol{1}$,
        $\boldsymbol{c}^{(0)}{=}\boldsymbol{0}$. For
        $t{=}1,\dots,T_{\mathrm{outer}}{=}8$: the
        \textbf{$\boldsymbol{c}$-update} runs $T_c$ FISTA (or $K$ LISTA)
        steps on
        $\boldsymbol{A}_{\!\mathrm{eff}}(\boldsymbol{g}^{(t-1)})$; the
        \textbf{$\boldsymbol{g}$-update} solves
        $(\boldsymbol{B}^{\!\top}\!\boldsymbol{B}{+}\lambda_g\boldsymbol{L})\boldsymbol{g}{=}\boldsymbol{B}^{\!\top}\!\boldsymbol{y}$
        in closed form, clamping to $[\epsilon_g,\infty)$.

        \heading{Closed-form $\boldsymbol{g}$-step}
        With $\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}{=}\bm{\Psi}\boldsymbol{c}$ the model
        is linear in $\boldsymbol{g}$,
        $\boldsymbol{y}{=}\boldsymbol{B}(\hat{\boldsymbol{s}})\boldsymbol{g}{+}\boldsymbol{w}$,
        giving
        \[
          \boldsymbol{g}^\star
          =\bigl(\boldsymbol{B}^{\!\top}\!\boldsymbol{B}+\lambda_g \boldsymbol{L}\bigr)^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{y},
        \]
        with $\boldsymbol{L}{=}\nabla^{\!\top}\!\nabla$ the 1D discrete
        Laplacian. The scalar invariance
        $(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{g})\!\to\!(a\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{g}/a)$
        is resolved at evaluation by the optimal positive gain
        $a^\star{=}\langle\hat{\boldsymbol{s}},\boldsymbol{s}_{\mathrm{true}}\rangle/\|\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}\|^2$,
        applied identically to baselines.

        \heading{Deep-unfolded LISTA inner solver}
        Replace the FISTA $\boldsymbol{c}$-step with $K$ learned layers
        \citep{gregor2010lista}:
        \[
          \boldsymbol{c}^{(k+1)}
          =\mathcal S_{\theta_k}\!\bigl(
          \boldsymbol{W}_t\boldsymbol{c}^{(k)}+\boldsymbol{W}_e\boldsymbol{y}\bigr),
          \quad k=0,\dots,K{-}1.
        \]
        Initialise
        $\boldsymbol{W}_e{=}\tfrac1L(\boldsymbol{A}\bm{\Psi})^{\!\top}$,
        $\boldsymbol{W}_t{=}\boldsymbol{I}{-}\tfrac1L(\boldsymbol{A}\bm{\Psi})^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{A}\bm{\Psi}$,
        $\theta_k{=}\lambda/L$ to reproduce ISTA at step zero, then
        train end-to-end on $(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{c})$ pairs.

        \heading{Stability tricks}
        Naive Adam at $5{\times}10^{-3}$ produces a clean descent to
        $0.12$ NMSE, then blows up to $\sim\!10^3$. Diagnosing,
        $\rho(\boldsymbol{W}_t){>}1$ along some eigendirection causes the
        recurrence to diverge. The fix combines (i) lowering
        lr$\to\!10^{-3}$, (ii) gradient clip-norm $1.0$, and
        (iii) restoring the best-validation checkpoint.

        \heading{LISTA vs.\ classical at matched compute}
        \textbf{Setup.} $K{=}10$ tied-weight LISTA trained on $5000$

      \begin{block}{Limitations}

        \heading{Failure mode: near-uniform scenes}
        Moon's centre patch is mostly black sky after $8{\times}$ gradient
        multiplication, so the first c-step has too little scene energy;
        noise then dominates the gain update and drives $\boldsymbol{g}$
        to a noise-fitting solution.
        The sequential pipeline avoids this by \emph{never} estimating
        $\boldsymbol{g}$ -- its column-mean divisor is essentially a copy
        of the gradient. \textbf{When the scene cannot identify
        $\boldsymbol{g}$, refusing to estimate it is a virtue.}
        A $\kappa(\boldsymbol{B}^{\!\top}\!\boldsymbol{B})$ gate was tested
        and falsified. \textbf{Deployment rule:} gate on the first c-step's
        scene energy $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}\|_2$ and fall back to sequential
        when it is too small.

        \heading{Other limitations}
        The 1D illumination model suits horizontal gradients (2D
        vignettes need a richer prior); synthetic-trained LISTA needs
        distribution-wide noise training; coded-aperture validation
        and outer-loop unfolding are future work.

      \end{block}
